What is GIP and How Does It Work?

Gastric Inhibitory Polypeptide (GIP) is a hormone secreted by the intestine in response to food intake. Research suggests that GIP plays a dual role in metabolism by promoting insulin secretion and inhibiting gastric motility, which helps to control appetite. By influencing how our bodies respond to food, GIP can help regulate hunger signals and reduce the likelihood of cravings.

The Role of GIP in Managing Cravings

Studies indicate that GIP can help mitigate cravings by enhancing feelings of fullness and satisfaction after meals. When levels of GIP are elevated, individuals may experience fewer urges to snack between meals, making it easier to stick to a healthy eating plan. This is particularly beneficial for those struggling with weight management, as controlling cravings is crucial for success.
